Gas vs Heat Pumps: Why We Are Phasing Out Gas Boilers in Sicilian Renovations

By Arch. Fabio Costanzo, Studio 4e · Updated: March 2026

Urgent question? Call: +39 329 973 6697

The question of gas boiler vs heat pump comes up in almost every Sicilian renovation brief we receive. The answer in 2026 is almost always heat pump — not primarily for environmental reasons, but because the financial case has shifted decisively: heat pumps are 3–4× more energy-efficient than gas boilers, gas prices in Italy have risen 60% since 2021, and EU regulations are progressively restricting new gas boiler installations. This is not a future trend; it is a current project decision.

The efficiency comparison: why heat pumps beat gas in Sicily's climate

A condensing gas boiler converts gas energy to heat at approximately 93–98% efficiency — so 1 kWh of gas produces about 0.95 kWh of heat. A modern air-source heat pump moves heat from outside to inside rather than generating it, achieving a Coefficient of Performance (COP) of 3.0–4.5 in Sicily's winter temperatures. This means 1 kWh of electricity produces 3–4.5 kWh of heat.

The comparison in Sicily's climate:

  • Gas boiler: to produce 10,000 kWh of heat, you burn 10,500 kWh of gas (at 95% efficiency)
  • Heat pump: to produce 10,000 kWh of heat, you consume 2,500–3,300 kWh of electricity (at COP 3–4)

At 2026 Italian energy prices (natural gas approximately €1.10/kWh, electricity approximately €0.25/kWh including network and taxes), the annual energy cost comparison for a 150 sqm property requiring 10,000 kWh/year of heating:

SystemEnergy consumedAnnual cost
Gas condensing boiler10,500 kWh gas€1,155/year
Heat pump (COP 3.5)2,860 kWh electricity€715/year
Heat pump + solar PV (50% self-supply)1,430 kWh grid electricity€358/year

The heat pump annual saving: €440/year. Combined with solar PV: €797/year. Over a 20-year project life, the present-value energy savings easily justify the higher equipment cost.

Installation costs: heat pump vs gas boiler comparison

The main argument for gas boilers has always been lower upfront cost. This remains partially true but is narrowing:

  • Gas condensing boiler + radiators (150 sqm property): €5,000–8,000 installed, including boiler (25kW), hydraulic circuit, radiators in 5–6 rooms, thermostatic controls.
  • Air-source heat pump + underfloor heating (150 sqm property): €14,000–22,000 installed, including heat pump (10–14 kW), underfloor heating circuits with manifolds, buffer tank, controls. Higher upfront cost, substantially lower running cost.
  • Air-source heat pump + fan-coil units (simpler retrofit): €9,000–15,000 installed, using fan-coil units instead of underfloor heating. Better retrofit option when underfloor heating is not feasible.

The payback period for the heat pump premium (compared to gas): at €440/year annual saving, the additional €9,000 in upfront cost pays back in approximately 20 years. With solar PV savings, the payback shortens to 11–12 years. These paybacks are borderline marginal — the financial case alone is not decisive.

The decisive factors are non-financial: gas supply availability (many rural Sicilian properties have no gas network connection), regulatory direction (EU regulation 2024/1610 restricts new gas boiler installations from 2025 in new buildings and from 2040 for replacements), and the asset value impact of a heat-pump-ready, gas-free property on the European luxury property market.

The EU regulatory direction: what is actually required in 2026

The Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD, recast 2024) and the related EU delegated acts establish a clear direction:

  • New buildings from 2025: new residential buildings must achieve near-zero energy performance (NZEB standards). New gas boiler installations in new buildings are effectively prohibited in most EU member states under NZEB requirements — heat pump or district heating are required.
  • Existing buildings being renovated: from 2026–2028, Italy (and other EU members) must implement requirements that renovation projects achieve a defined energy class improvement. Renovating to class A or B typically means heat pump + building envelope insulation, not gas boiler replacement.
  • Gas boiler ban for replacements (2040 target): the political direction is clear, though implementation details are still being finalised in Italy via legislative decree. Buying a gas boiler today means owning a system that will need replacement within 15–20 years under a regulatory framework that will not allow like-for-like replacement.

The solar PV + heat pump combination: the optimal Sicilian specification

Sicily's solar resource — 2,000–2,200 hours of sunshine per year on average, peaking at 5–6 kWh/m²/day in July — makes solar PV + heat pump the strongest financial and environmental combination for any property with available roof area:

  • A 6 kWp roof solar system produces approximately 8,500 kWh/year in Palermo (9,200 kWh/year in Agrigento, the most solar-intensive province)
  • Self-consumption of solar electricity by the heat pump reduces net grid electricity consumption. With a home battery (10 kWh storage), self-consumption rate rises from 30% to 55–65%
  • Combined annual savings (heat pump + solar + battery vs gas + no solar): €800–1,200/year for a 150 sqm property

The solar PV installation: €9,000–15,000 for a 6 kWp system with Conto Energia-compliant design and Terna grid connection. Battery storage: €6,000–9,000 for a 10 kWh lithium system. Total solar + battery: €15,000–24,000, with a net investment after state incentives (Bonus Casa 50% deduction or Conto Termico grant) of €8,000–15,000.

What to specify when briefing an MEP engineer in Sicily

For a renovation brief to a mechanical, electrical and plumbing engineer (progettista degli impianti):

  • Request R290 (propane refrigerant) heat pump models — these are Regulation 517/2014 F-gas compliant and preferred by forward-looking installers
  • Specify variable-speed compressor (inverter) technology for best part-load efficiency — not on-off compressor systems
  • Include MVHR (Ventilazione Meccanica Controllata con Recupero di Calore) in the brief — heat pump systems work best when combined with controlled ventilation, which in Sicilian summer months also provides pre-cooling via the heat exchanger
  • Specify integration with smart home / KNX for tariff optimization — programming the heat pump to run during low-cost electricity hours (usually night-time) and during peak solar PV production
