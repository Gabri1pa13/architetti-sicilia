Marzamemi and the Coast: Buying Heritage Fishermen's Cottages and Tonnara

By Arch. Fabio Costanzo, Studio 4e · Updated: March 2026

Urgent question? Call: +39 329 973 6697

Marzamemi, on Siracusa's southeastern coast, is one of Sicily's most photographed fishing villages — the piazza, the tonnara (tuna fishing compound), and the case dei pescatori stretching along the shore. Its popularity has made it expensive relative to its inland neighbours. It has also made due diligence essential: the historic cottages that appear in Instagram feeds have flood risk classifications, heritage restrictions, and maintenance costs that buyers need to understand before signing.

What makes Marzamemi architecturally and historically significant

Marzamemi was developed in the early 18th century by the Villadorata family of Noto as a private fishing village organised around tuna processing. The Tonnara di Marzamemi (the tuna processing facility) is one of the most intact examples of the Arabic-derived mattanza fishing tradition in Sicily. The village plan — a central piazza flanked by case dei pescatori (fishermen's terraced houses) in a unified architectural composition — was entirely designed and built as a coherent settlement, not organically developed.

This designed unity gives Marzamemi its architectural coherence and also explains its planning constraints: the entire village centre is classified as centro storico under the PRG di Pachino (the municipal authority), subject to Zone A conservation requirements, and the Tonnara complex is under a Soprintendenza di Siracusa individual vincolo monumentale as an immovable heritage asset of exceptional cultural significance.

Properties purchased within Marzamemi's historic perimeter are therefore subject to: PRG Zone A restrictions (risanamento conservativo or restauro only), Soprintendenza nulla osta for external works, and proximity to the Tonnara's individual vincolo which may extend to nearby properties. The standard due diligence sequence (check PRG zone, request building permit file, commission architectural survey to verify stato legittimo) applies here with particular urgency given the concentrated heritage classification.

Flood risk in Marzamemi: the PAI and coastal dynamics

Marzamemi sits on a low limestone shelf at an elevation of 1–3 metres above sea level. The eastern Mediterranean coast at this latitude is exposed to storm surges from the southeast (the bora and libeccio winds drive significant sea states in winter). The PAI classification for much of the Marzamemi coastal zone is R2–R3 flood risk.

The practical implications for property buyers:

  • Ground-floor properties on the seafront side of the village are periodically subject to sea flooding during winter storm events. The 2018 and 2022 storms affected several ground-floor commercial properties on the piazza and the seafront promenade.
  • Ground-floor electrical installations, boilers, and service equipment must be positioned above the flood level (typically 0.5–0.8m above floor level in R2 zones, higher in R3).
  • Some properties in R3 zones may face planning restrictions on significant works or change of use, particularly where the proposed use would increase overnight occupancy (and therefore flood evacuation risk).
  • Insurance for ground-floor properties in R3 zones requires specific flood endorsements and is priced at a significant premium. Verify insurability before purchase.

The tonnara and its conversion limits

The Tonnara di Marzamemi is a property complex (currently under long-term lease from the regional authority that manages it following the Florio family's withdrawal from tuna processing in the 1970s). It periodically hosts cultural events and has been the subject of conversion discussions for hotel or cultural use. The vincolo monumentale on the Tonnara prevents any significant modification to the structure or use that would alter its character as a historic industrial facility.

For buyers interested in the Tonnara itself or in immediately adjacent buildings: be aware that the vincolo creates a "shadow zone" of heightened scrutiny on any property within sight lines of the Tonnara's principal facades. The Soprintendenza may condition works on adjacent properties that in other contexts would require only a standard nulla osta, requiring instead a full OUV-style impact assessment demonstrating no adverse effect on the Tonnara's setting.

The real estate market: fishermen's cottages vs restored modern villas

Marzamemi has two distinct property sub-markets:

Historic case dei pescatori: the terraced cottages on the piazza and adjacent streets. Typically 50–90 sqm, 2–3 rooms, stone construction with original beam ceilings. Unrenovated: €60,000–120,000. Well-renovated to boutique standard: €180,000–320,000. Short-term rental potential: €150–280/night in high season, strong demand from Italian and European visitors. Planning constraints: maximum conservation, no external extensions, full Soprintendenza involvement for facade works.

Modern villas on the coastal strip (south of Marzamemi): newer residential construction on limestone plots with sea views. 100–200 sqm, private gardens, private or shared pools. More recent construction: €250,000–500,000 depending on finishes and sea proximity. Fewer heritage constraints but coastal vincolo and PAI flood risk considerations still apply. Lower character appeal to the boutique rental market.

The investment case for a case dei pescatori is stronger than for a modern villa, assuming correct renovation and permitted use. The heritage context is the asset — it is why guests choose Marzamemi over more accessible coastal towns, and why well-executed boutique conversions consistently outperform modern villa rentals on a per-sqm basis.

The summer season economics

Marzamemi's rental market is intensely seasonal — the vast majority of visitor stays occur June–September, with a modest shoulder season in May and October. Annual occupancy for short-term rentals in Marzamemi typically reaches 35–50% when measured across all months, concentrated in 90–100 high-season days. Annual gross rental revenue for a well-marketed, renovated 2-bedroom casa dei pescatori: €20,000–35,000. Typical operating costs (cleaning, maintenance, utilities, platform fees, property management): €6,000–12,000. Net yield on total invested capital (purchase + renovation): 4–7% for a competently executed project.

The risks to this revenue: climate disruption is extending seasonal patterns (October is increasingly warm and busy), but jellyfish (medusa) infestations in July–August have reduced beach appeal in some recent years on this section of the Ionian coast. Monitor the balance of positive trends (longer season) against negative ones (water quality, overcrowding management by the municipality) in your investment projections.

Getting the CIR code and the accesso ai dati sul turismo

As with all Sicilian tourist properties, a CIR (Codice Identificativo Regionale) is required for short-term rental. For a property in Marzamemi, this requires: planning conformity (conformità urbanistica), agibilità, energy certificate (APE), and registration with the Comune di Pachino's tourist accommodation register. Most of this documentation is straightforward for a correctly-permitted property; the issue arises when the property has inherited irregularities (common in the historic village where works were carried out by previous generations without permits). Resolve all irregularities before attempting CIR registration.
