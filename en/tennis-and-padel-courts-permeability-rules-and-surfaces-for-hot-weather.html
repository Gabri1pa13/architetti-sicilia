Tennis and Padel Courts in Sicily: Permeability Rules and Surfaces for Hot Weather

By Arch. Fabio Costanzo, Studio 4e · Updated: March 2026

Urgent question? Call: +39 329 973 6697

A padel court or tennis court on a Sicilian estate is a planning matter — not just a construction one. Italian building regulations classify sports courts as urban works (opere di urbanizzazione secondaria), which require permits and, in areas with soil permeability requirements, specific drainage solutions. In Sicily's summer heat, the surface material choice also directly determines playability and maintenance cost.

The permit path for a tennis or padel court

A full-size tennis court (23.77 × 10.97m = 261 sqm) or padel court (20 × 10m = 200 sqm) is classified as a new construction work under Italian planning law and requires a building permit. The specific permit type depends on the PRG zone:

  • Agricultural zone (Zone E in PRG): outdoor sports courts for private use are classified as pertinenze (appurtenances) to the main residential building, subject to size limits defined in the PRG. Many Sicilian rural PRG plans allow sports courts up to 200–300 sqm as pertinenze, sometimes requiring that the court be associated with the main residential unit and not constitute an independent commercial sports facility.
  • Residential zone (Zone B, C): permesso di costruire required for new impermeable surfacing above a defined area threshold. The specific threshold varies by municipality, but the principal issue in residential zones is the impermeable surface contribution to the stormwater runoff regime.
  • Protected zones (vincolo paesaggistico): courts within the 150m coastal strip or in landscape-protected areas require Soprintendenza nulla osta in addition to the municipal permit. The visual impact of a court on a protected landscape (bright green artificial turf, white court lines, perimeter fencing) is frequently contested.

Soil permeability requirements: the key technical issue

Italian legislation on soil permeability (principio di invarianza idraulica, established progressively from 2006 with Regione Siciliana implementation in specific provinces) requires that new impermeable surfaces on private land do not increase the rate of stormwater runoff from the site compared to the pre-intervention state. A tennis court (261 sqm of impermeable surface) creates a significant runoff increase if no mitigation is applied.

The mitigation options accepted by most Sicilian SUE offices:

  • Retention basin: a concrete or earthwork basin (vasche di laminazione) sized to store the peak runoff from the court during a design storm event (typically the 2–year return period, 1-hour storm, which in Sicily produces approximately 35–50mm of rainfall). For a 261 sqm court, this requires approximately 10–15 m³ of storage — a tank approximately 3m × 2m × 2m deep.
  • Permeable perimeter strip: French drains (drenaggi a trincea) around the court perimeter intercept runoff before it leaves the property. Must be designed to handle the court drainage hydraulic capacity.
  • Permeable surface type: some modern court surfaces (acrylic on open-grade base, artificial turf with sand infill on permeable sub-base) are classified as semi-permeable, which reduces the runoff coefficient and therefore reduces the required mitigation volume. Ask the surface supplier for the permeability coefficient before specifying.

Surface materials for Sicily's climate

Surface selection for a Sicilian outdoor court is significantly influenced by the climate: 60+ days above 30°C per year, intense UV, and the possibility of playing from October to May even in warm years. The options:

  • Acrylic hardcourt (cushion or standard): the most common surface for private Sicilian courts. Advantages: durable, consistent bounce, low maintenance, adaptable to padel or tennis layouts. Disadvantages: absorbs heat (surface can reach 55–60°C in full sun in July–August, making afternoon play unpleasant without shade). Standard acrylic without UV-stabilised pigment degrades within 5–7 years in Sicilian sun; specify UV-stabilised acrylic specifically (Plexipave, Laykold Elite, or equivalent). Cost installed: €80–120/sqm for a cushioned surface; €40–65/sqm for standard acrylic.
  • Clay (terra battuta): the traditional Italian and European court surface. Lower heat absorption than acrylic (cooler underfoot), slower ball bounce (more forgiving). Major disadvantage: requires daily watering and weekly maintenance (rolling, line remarking) — an irrigation system and committed maintenance regime are prerequisites. Not suitable for occasional-use private courts. Cost installed: €50–80/sqm; maintenance: €3,000–6,000/year with professional maintenance service.
  • Artificial turf (manto erboso sintetico): increasingly popular for padel courts in Sicily. Heat profile intermediate between acrylic and clay. Requires sand or rubber granule infill (specified by the surface manufacturer). The recent environmental push away from crumb rubber (SBR) infill, due to microplastic concerns, has increased use of silica sand + EPDM cork alternatives. Cost: €55–85/sqm installed. Replacement cycle: 8–12 years depending on UV stabilisation quality.

Padel vs tennis: structural differences in the court enclosure

A padel court requires a full perimeter enclosure of glass panels (back walls and side walls) and metal grid fencing (lateral upper sections). The structural frame (typically hot-dip galvanised steel) must be designed to resist wind loads — a critical consideration for exposed hilltop properties in Sicily where the maestrale wind reaches 80–100 km/h in winter storms.

Wind load calculation per NTC 2018: for a padel court in Zone 3 (central Sicily, the most exposed inland areas), the wind pressure on the glass panel back wall can reach 1.2–1.5 kN/m². For a 10m wide × 4m tall glass wall, the total wind force is 12–15 tonnes. The foundation anchoring of the court frame must be designed for this load by a structural engineer. A standard padel court frame intended for sheltered urban locations (the default product from most suppliers) is not adequate for exposed hilltop sites without additional engineering.

Padel court enclosure costs: standard steel-and-glass court enclosure (20 × 10m, standard exposure): €18,000–28,000. Reinforced frame for high-wind exposure: add €5,000–10,000 for engineering and upgraded steel section sizes.

Lighting for evening play

Court lighting in a rural Sicilian context may require a light pollution assessment if the property is in or near a protected natural area (Parco dell'Etna, Riserva Naturale Monte Cofano, etc.). Italian law L. 10/1991 and subsequent regional implementing acts regulate external lighting levels in sensitive zones. LED floodlights with controlled optics (full cut-off, no upward light) at 300–500 lux on the court surface are the standard specification. Pole height: 8–10m for a tennis court, 6–8m for padel. LED system with 4 poles: €12,000–20,000 installed, with KNX or timer control for occupancy-based switching.

All-in cost summary for a padel court in rural Sicily

  • Structural engineering (wind load, foundation design): €2,500–5,000
  • Permit (SCIA or Permesso di Costruire): €1,500–4,000
  • Earthworks and drainage: €5,000–15,000
  • Court frame and enclosure (steel + glass/grid): €20,000–35,000
  • Surface (acrylic or artificial turf): €12,000–20,000
  • Lighting: €12,000–20,000
  • Total: €55,000–100,000 depending on exposure class and specification
