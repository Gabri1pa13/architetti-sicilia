Marsala Countryside: Converting Old Baglio Wineries into Boutique Hotels

By Arch. Fabio Costanzo, Studio 4e · Updated: March 2026

Urgent question? Call: +39 329 973 6697

The bagli of western Sicily — the enclosed farm-courtyard complexes built around wine production, salt processing, and agricultural storage from the 17th to early 20th centuries — are the architectural typology most sought after for boutique hotel conversion in western Sicily. The combination of authentic character, large footprint, and rural setting is exactly what the luxury agri-hospitality market demands. The conversion process, however, navigates agriturismo law, hotel classification, food service licensing, and heritage planning — often simultaneously.

What a baglio is and what determines its conversion potential

The Sicilian baglio (from Arabic bāhiya, enclosure) is a self-contained agricultural complex organised around a central courtyard (cortile), with a main residential wing (corpo padronale), worker quarters (dependanze), wine production building (palmento), storage cellars (cantine), and chapel in larger examples. The typology is most concentrated in the Marsala-Trapani axis, in the Agrigento hinterland, and in the Ragusa wine zone.

A baglio's conversion potential depends on:

  • Footprint and room count: a minimum viable boutique hotel requires 8–12 guest rooms; this needs a total built footprint of at least 800–1,200 sqm. Many smaller bagli (500–700 sqm) cannot accommodate enough rooms for economic viability without volume expansion, which heritage planning may restrict.
  • PRG zone and agricultural classification: bagli outside urban centres are typically in Zone E (agricultural) of the municipal PRG. Commercial hotel use in Zone E requires a change-of-use (cambio di destinazione d'uso) from agricultural/residential to tourist/hotel use, which is not always permitted by the PRG.
  • Soprintendenza and vincolo status: many bagli over 70 years old are subject to the Codice dei Beni Culturali. Significant renovation, volume additions, and especially changes to the courtyard configuration are subject to Soprintendenza approval.
  • Agricultural land and the agriturismo threshold: for bagli being converted as agriturismo, the associated agricultural land must be sufficient to meet the income ratio requirement. A baglio surrounded by 10 hectares of DOC vineyards meets the test easily; a baglio with 0.5 hectares of abandoned orchard does not.

The three conversion pathways: agriturismo, albergo diffuso, or hotel

Pathway 1: Agriturismo

The most permissive planning framework for rural hotel conversion. Up to 30 beds (50 with special authorisation) in structures associated with active agricultural production. Food service limited to products of the farm — menus must be dominated by estate-produced ingredients. No swimming pool or spa can be classified as agriturismo appurtenance without specific PRG authorisation. Advantage: lower minimum standards than a hotel, agricultural tax regime, reduced TARI waste tariff. Disadvantage: must maintain genuine agricultural activity; cannot simply be a hotel that sells wine.

Pathway 2: Albergo Diffuso (distributed hotel)

An Italian concept where accommodation is distributed across multiple buildings in a village or rural complex, with a common reception and breakfast facility. The albergo diffuso classification (regulated by Legge Regionale Siciliana n.27/1985 and updated in 2012) allows the guest rooms to be in separate buildings — ideal for a baglio's typology where worker quarters and dependanze are physically separate from the main building. Minimum standards: 1-star equivalent infrastructure (private bathrooms in each unit, reception accessible 16 hours/day, breakfast service). Higher minimum investment but avoids the agricultural income constraint of agriturismo.

Pathway 3: Hotel (albergo) with change-of-use

Full hotel classification requires a formal cambio di destinazione d'uso from the Comune, approval by the Regione Assessorato Regionale del Turismo, fire safety certificate (Certificato Prevenzione Incendi — CPI from the Vigili del Fuoco), and compliance with hotel minimum standards (D.P.R. 616/1977 and Legge Regionale 27/1985 classification grids). This is the highest compliance barrier but gives maximum operating freedom — no agricultural income constraint, unlimited food service, full branding as a hotel rather than agriturismo.

The fire safety certificate (CPI) for historic bagli

The CPI is typically the most technically demanding permit for a baglio hotel conversion. The Vigili del Fuoco (fire brigade) apply D.M. 9 aprile 1994 (fire safety regulations for hotels) which requires:

  • Maximum travel distance to an emergency exit of 30–45m (depending on floor level)
  • Minimum corridor width of 1.2m for evacuation
  • Fire-rated compartmentation between sleeping areas and common areas (REI 60 minimum)
  • Automatic fire detection (rivelatori di fumo) in all sleeping areas and corridors
  • Emergency lighting (luci di emergenza) on all evacuation routes
  • Fire suppression: sprinkler systems required for hotels above 25 guest rooms or in buildings over 24m height

Historic bagli with open courtyard circulation, wooden roof structures, and interconnected buildings present specific challenges for fire compartmentation that require creative engineering solutions. One common approach: sacrificial fire walls (pareti tagliafuoco) at building wing junctions that contain a fire while preserving the historic courtyard character. These must be designed by a fire safety engineer (tecnico antincendio iscritto negli elenchi del Corpo Nazionale dei Vigili del Fuoco).

The food service licence (SCIA alimentare)

Any hotel providing breakfast or restaurant service must hold a SCIA alimentare (food safety notification) under D.Lgs 193/2007 (HACCP regulations). For a boutique hotel with a restaurant, additional requirements include: food storage temperatures monitoring, staff food hygiene certification (HACCP training), and kitchen design compliance with Regolamento CE 852/2004 food hygiene standards.

A converted baglio kitchen must meet these standards — which typically means a complete new kitchen installation with stainless steel surfaces, correct hot/cold separation, hand washing station at entry, and mechanical exhaust ventilation. Heritage kitchens with original stone sinks, wooden shelving, and charm are incompatible with HACCP standards as working kitchens, though they can be retained as decorative elements adjacent to the functional kitchen.

Timeline and costs for a full baglio hotel conversion

For a 12-room boutique hotel in a 1,200 sqm baglio in the Marsala area:

  • Pre-design due diligence (PRG, vincolo, market study): €5,000–8,000
  • Architectural and engineering design (full project package): €40,000–70,000
  • Permits (Comune, Soprintendenza, VVF, Regione Turismo): 18–30 months, €8,000–15,000 in fees
  • Construction works (renovation + fitting out 12 rooms + restaurant + pool): €1,800,000–2,800,000
  • Equipment and FF&E (furniture, fixtures, kitchen, tech): €150,000–300,000
  • Total project investment: €2,000,000–3,200,000

Revenue capacity at typical boutique Sicily rates (€180–350/night per room, 60% annual occupancy): €600,000–1,000,000 gross revenue/year. EBITDA margin for a well-operated 12-room property: 25–35%. The financial case is viable for the right baglio, but the permitting timeline (18–30 months) must be factored into the return-on-investment calculation from day one.
